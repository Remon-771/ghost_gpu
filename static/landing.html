01 — The problem

A dead job that still looks alive

In a shared GPU lab, the signal everyone trusts is "is the GPU busy?" But a job whose gradients exploded into NaN, or whose data loader deadlocked, keeps issuing GPU work and keeps reporting high utilisation. It never crashes.

So it burns electricity and blocks the queue for hours — while four other researchers wait behind a job that died at 2 a.m. Nobody notices, because everyone is watching whether the GPU is busy, not whether the work is alive.

GhostGPU watches the pulse of each job — like a patient monitor in a hospital — and raises the alarm the moment the work stops, even while the GPU looks perfectly busy.

02 — How it works

Collect the signals, judge them, act

GhostGPU only reads telemetry the cluster already produces. No researcher changes their code.

📡
STEP 01

Collect

Reads GPU utilisation, loss, and checkpoint activity per minute — the vital signs of every running job.

🧠
STEP 02

Detect

A two-stage model learns what healthy training looks like and scores how far each job has drifted from it.

⚡
STEP 03

Act

Verdicts go live — HEALTHY / SUSPICIOUS / GHOST — with the money burned and one-click "kill & free".

Stage 1 · unsupervised

Isolation Forest

Trained only on healthy jobs — flags anything unfamiliar, no failure labels needed.

→
Stage 2 · supervised

XGBoost + calibration

Turns the signals into a calibrated ghost probability with a plain-English reason.

→
Safety

Hysteresis

A job must stay abnormal across several windows before it's called a ghost — so no flip-flopping.
